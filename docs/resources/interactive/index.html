Section 01 · Economic surface

Cobb–Douglas production surface

This is an actual computed surface—not a decorative polyhedron. The canvas evaluates Y = KαZ1−α on a grid of capital and productivity values and projects that grid into a rotatable wireframe view.

Y(K,Z) = K^α Z^(1−α)

Interpretation: changing α changes the curvature and relative sensitivity to capital. The visualization is deterministic and has no hidden simulation state.

Cobb-Douglas production surface. Use the capital-share and rotation controls to change the view.

Section 03 · Stylized simulation

Decentralized bids, asks & transaction-price discovery

Buyers and sellers move in a bounded arena. When opposite sides meet and the buyer bid is at least the seller ask, a transaction occurs at their midpoint. The displayed price is an exponentially smoothed transaction-price estimate, not a solved Walrasian clearing price.

Pedagogical boundary: this illustrates decentralized price discovery only. It is not calibrated market microstructure and does not establish equilibrium.

Section 04 · Auditable workflows

From equation to code to diagnostic

These are semantic workflow summaries rendered as ordinary HTML so they remain readable with scripts disabled, on narrow screens, and in assistive technology.

Structural VAR identification

  1. Estimate reduced-form VAR.
  2. State identifying restrictions.
  3. Map reduced-form to structural shocks.
  4. Compute impulse responses.
  5. Check sensitivity to ordering/restrictions.

Box–Jenkins time series

  1. Inspect transformations and stationarity evidence.
  2. Select candidate lag orders.
  3. Estimate models.
  4. Check residual dependence.
  5. Evaluate forecasts out of sample.

Aiyagari equilibrium

  1. Guess interest rate.
  2. Recover wage from the firm problem.
  3. Solve household Bellman problem.
  4. Compute invariant distribution and capital supply.
  5. Compare supply with firm capital demand and report residual.

Fama–MacBeth regression

  1. Estimate time-series betas.
  2. Run cross-sectional regressions by period.
  3. Average risk premia.
  4. Use appropriate standard errors.
  5. Report sensitivity to factor definitions and sample.
