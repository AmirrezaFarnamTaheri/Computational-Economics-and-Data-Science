# Micro Models — Bibliography

> Pathway in one line: from preference primitives to mechanisms you could actually run.

## Reading pathway

1. Mas-Colell, Whinston & Green — the shared language of the module.
2. Fudenberg & Tirole — game theory with the right level of rigor.
3. Berry, Levinsohn & Pakes (1995) with Nevo (2000) — structural demand in
   theory and in practice.
4. Milgrom (2004) — auction theory at work.
5. Roth & Sotomayor (1990) — matching when markets need a designer.

## Seminal

- **Arrow & Debreu (1954).** *Existence of an Equilibrium for a Competitive
  Economy.* Econometrica. — Walrasian equilibrium made a theorem.
- **Nash (1950; 1951).** *Equilibrium Points in n-Person Games* (PNAS);
  *Non-Cooperative Games* (Annals of Mathematics). — The equilibrium concept
  and its existence proof.
- **Vickrey (1961).** *Counterspeculation, Auctions, and Competitive Sealed
  Tenders.* Journal of Finance. — Auction theory and mechanism design begin.
- **McFadden (1974).** *Conditional Logit Analysis of Qualitative Choice
  Behavior.* In: Frontiers in Econometrics. — Random utility meets data.
- **Mirrlees (1971).** *An Exploration in the Theory of Optimum Income
  Taxation.* Review of Economic Studies. — Adverse selection and optimal
  mechanisms; the moral-hazard/adverse-selection split used in lectures 05–06.

## Modern

- **Mas-Colell, Whinston & Green (1995).** *Microeconomic Theory.* Oxford
  University Press. — The canonical graduate text; chapters map to lectures.
- **Berry, Levinsohn & Pakes (1995).** *Automobile Prices in Market
  Equilibrium.* Econometrica. — Random-coefficient demand; the BLP machine of
  lecture 07.
- **Nevo (2000).** *A Practitioner's Guide to Estimation of Random-Coefficients
  Logit Demand Models.* Journal of Economics & Management Strategy. — How to
  actually run BLP without weeping.
- **Milgrom (2004).** *Putting Auction Theory to Work.* Cambridge University
  Press. — From revenue equivalence to spectrum auctions.

## Theoretical

- **Fudenberg & Tirole (1991).** *Game Theory.* MIT Press. — Dynamic games,
  refinement, and repeated interaction with proofs.
- **Roth & Sotomayor (1990).** *Two-Sided Matching.* Cambridge University
  Press. — Stable matching theory behind market design.
