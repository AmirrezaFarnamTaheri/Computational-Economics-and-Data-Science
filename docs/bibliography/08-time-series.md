# Time Series — Bibliography

> Pathway in one line: from describing dynamics to trusting forecasts.

## Reading pathway

1. Box & Jenkins — the identification-estimation-diagnosis discipline.
2. Hamilton — the graduate-standard reference for every model in the module.
3. Brockwell & Davis — the time-domain theory underneath.
4. Kilian & Lütkepohl — modern structural VAR practice.
5. Francq & Zakoïan — GARCH theory at full depth.

## Seminal

- **Yule (1927).** *On a Method of Investigating Periodicities in Disturbed
  Series...* Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A. — The first
  autoregression (sunspots); AR models begin here.
- **Box & Jenkins (1970).** *Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control.*
  Holden-Day. — ARIMA as an industrial process.
- **Granger (1969).** *Investigating Causal Relations by Econometric Models and
  Cross-spectral Methods.* Econometrica. — Granger causality.
- **Sims (1980).** *Macroeconomics and Reality.* Econometrica. — VARs against
  "incredible" identifying restrictions.
- **Engle (1982).** *Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity...*
  Econometrica. — ARCH; volatility becomes forecastable.
- **Engle & Granger (1987).** *Co-Integration and Error Correction.*
  Econometrica. — Cointegration's two-step foundation.
- **Dickey & Fuller (1979).** *Distribution of the Estimators for Autoregressive
  Time Series with a Unit Root.* JASA. — The unit-root test used everywhere.

## Modern

- **Hamilton (1994).** *Time Series Analysis.* Princeton University Press. —
  The graduate reference; every chapter is a lecture here.
- **Kilian & Lütkepohl (2017).** *Structural Vector Autoregressive Analysis.*
  Cambridge University Press. — Modern SVAR: identification, inference, and
  the small-sample caveats.
- **Francq & Zakoïan (2019).** *GARCH Models: Structure, Statistical Inference
  and Financial Applications*, 2nd ed. Wiley. — GARCH theory and QMLE rigor.

## Theoretical

- **Brockwell & Davis (1991).** *Time Series: Theory and Methods*, 2nd ed.
  Springer. — Hilbert-space treatment of prediction and spectral theory.
