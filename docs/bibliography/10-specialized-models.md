# Specialized Models — Bibliography

> Pathway in one line: emergence, networks, and planetary constraints — models where composition matters most.

## Reading pathway

1. Schelling (1971) — emergence from trivial rules.
2. Axelrod (1984) — cooperation as an evolved outcome.
3. Jackson (2008) — the network mathematics toolkit.
4. Farmer & Foley (2009) — the case for ABM in macro.
5. Nordhaus (1992; Nobel lecture 2018) — integrated assessment done carefully.

## Seminal

- **Schelling (1971).** *Dynamic Models of Segregation.* Journal of Conflict
  Resolution. — Mild preferences, extreme outcomes; the founding ABM result,
  originally done with coins and graph paper.
- **Axelrod (1984).** *The Evolution of Cooperation.* Basic Books. — Tournaments
  showing Tit-for-Tat's robustness; the experimental template for ABM labs.
- **Epstein & Axtell (1996).** *Growing Artificial Societies.* MIT Press. —
  Sugarscape: whole societies grown from bottom-up rules.
- **Nordhaus (1992).** *An Optimal Transition Path for Controlling Greenhouse
  Gases.* Science. — DICE: the first computable coupling of growth and climate.
- **Granovetter (1973).** *The Strength of Weak Ties.* American Journal of
  Sociology. — Network structure as economic substance.

## Modern

- **Jackson (2008).** *Social and Economic Networks.* Princeton University
  Press. — The standard graduate treatment of network games and diffusion.
- **Farmer & Foley (2009).** *The Economy Needs Agent-Based Modelling.* Nature.
  — The manifesto for ABM in macro policy analysis.
- **Hamill & Gilbert (2016).** *Agent-Based Modelling in Economics.* Sage. —
  A practical methods guide aligned with this module's workflow.

## Theoretical

- **Newman (2010).** *Networks: An Introduction.* Oxford University Press. —
  Centrality measures, random graphs, and percolation with derivations.
- **Aumann (1976).** *Agreeing to Disagree.* Annals of Statistics. — Common
  knowledge formalized; the epistemic base under repeated-game results.
